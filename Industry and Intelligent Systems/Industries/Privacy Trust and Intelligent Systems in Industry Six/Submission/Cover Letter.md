Dear Editor,

I submit the manuscript "Privacy, Trust & Intelligent Systems in Industry 6.0" for consideration as a research article in *Industries*.

The paper asks whether industrial sites can learn from each other's data under formal privacy and still certify where automated decisions stay within stated error rates. It introduces private aggregate risk control, in which sites train a shared model with differentially private federated averaging, release Skellam-noised histograms of calibration scores through secure aggregation and receive certified pass and alarm thresholds from a binomial-Skellam test. The paper proves validity under the privacy noise and derives the minimum number of recorded faults that any certificate needs. A frozen confirmatory protocol was evaluated on eight public industrial and security datasets, including CNC machining recordings from three machines and five sessions of an industrial control testbed.

Across 325 paired runs the method exceeded a 10 per cent miss-rate target once against 39 times for a private plug-in rule. The study also reports where the approach stops helping: rare-fault tasks fell below the evidence floor and returned decisions to people, differentially private training weakened rare-event ranking and drift on a gas turbine broke coverage. These results give plant and data-governance teams a concrete rule for choosing privacy budgets and sizing human review.

The manuscript is original, has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. Code, frozen protocol, derived data and every run output are openly available in the repository cited in the Data Availability Statement.
