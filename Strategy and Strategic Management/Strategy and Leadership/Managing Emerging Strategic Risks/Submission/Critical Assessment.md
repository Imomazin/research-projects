# Critical assessment from a reviewer's position

Assessed against the brief's Stage Six questions on 25 October 2026.

Research gap. The gap is supported by verified literature. Text-based risk measures in finance and accounting are built one category at a time (Hassan et al., 2019 and 2023; Sautner et al., 2023; Florackis et al., 2022) or as many separate exposures (Bao and Datta, 2014; Davis et al., 2020). The closest study, Neunsinger et al. (2026), links capability disclosure to share-price resilience in the pandemic. No verified study found by the Crossref searches combines a convergence count, a documented-response measure and operating outcomes across two shocks. A reviewer may still find a working paper the searches missed; the claim in the manuscript is worded as limited to the studies reviewed.

Theoretical contribution. The separation of disclosed exposure, documented action and realised sensitivity is clear and follows from the evidence. A reviewer could argue it is mainly an empirical caution. The Discussion ties it to dynamic capabilities, the attention-based view and decoupling, which gives it theoretical standing, but the paper does not develop a new construct with formal propositions.

Fit of design to questions. Strong for the descriptive question; adequate for the predictive questions. The episode design uses pre-shock disclosures and first-reported accounting values, so look-ahead bias is controlled.

Measurement validity. The main weakness. Dictionary precision rose to 0.94 pooled on a fresh sample after one revision, but supply-chain precision (14 of 20) and documented-response precision (36 of 60) are moderate and about 15 per cent of unclassified sentences contain category content. The audit was done by an AI assistant. Before submission two human coders should code the released samples so that agreement statistics can be reported. Measurement error biases associations towards zero, which matters for a paper whose main results are null.

Dataset integration. Defensible. Firm-year keys are CIK and Compustat-convention fiscal year; macro indices are aligned to each firm's fiscal year-end month. Revenue uses the largest revenue concept; liabilities and operating income have documented fallbacks.

Sample informativeness. About 680 firms per episode with roughly 50 industry clusters gives adequate power for moderate effects. Breadth confidence intervals in the pandemic exclude effects larger than about 0.03 log points per standard deviation, which supports a substantive null rather than an underpowered one.

Identification. No causal claims are made and none should be. The exposure-by-shock design assumes fiscal 2018 exposure was not chosen in anticipation of later shocks. Nominal revenue in 2021 to 2023 mixes price and volume; margins partly address this.

Do results support the conclusions? Yes for homogenisation and for the null on severity. The recovery association and the negative interaction are specification-dependent (they weaken under alternative materiality thresholds and diversity) and are presented as such.

Robustness. Proportionate: 23 pre-specified checks per association, both dictionaries, two error structures and an out-of-sample test. Wild-cluster bootstrap inference would strengthen the cross-sectional results given about 50 clusters.

Managerial implications. Follow from the findings and are hedged as requiring testing inside organisations. Table 6 links each recommendation to a result.

Journal fit. Good. The question is a leadership problem, the practitioner section is substantial and the paper engages recent Strategy & Leadership articles (Sheehan, 2020; Calandro, 2020; Day and Dennis, 2022; Schoemaker, 2022; Mortlock and Osiyevskyy, 2023; Dencik et al., 2023; Welch, 2026; Soleimani and Stauffer, 2026; Schoemaker and Truumees, 2026).

References. All 90 are generated from Crossref records retrieved by DOI (with OpenAlex completion) or from three manually verified records; the build fails if a citation lacks a verified record. One remembered DOI proved wrong and the work was dropped.

Journal requirements. Structured abstract of 229 words within the 250-word limit; Harvard-style citations; body about 5,300 words plus seven tables, five figures and references, within the preferred 7,000 to 9,000 words overall. The author guidelines page could not be read directly from this workspace; the word range and double-anonymised review were confirmed from the journal home page, and the abstract format follows Emerald's standard structure. Check the current guidelines in ScholarOne before submission.

Integrity notes. The repository is public, so the draft and data are visible before submission. Funding and competing-interest declarations are not in the manuscript and must be supplied by the author.
