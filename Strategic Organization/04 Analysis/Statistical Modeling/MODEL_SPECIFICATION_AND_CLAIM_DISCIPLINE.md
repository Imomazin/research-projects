# Model Specification and Claim Discipline

## Primary empirical objects
1. Conventional capability index: equal-weight mean of six baseline dimensions.
2. AI/Data capability index: equal-weight mean of four baseline dimensions.
3. Capability gap: Conventional minus AI/Data, participant-level within-person contrast.
4. ILO occupational exposure: official 2025 category linked through ISCO-08 career-interest coding.
5. High exposure: ILO Gradient 3 or Gradient 4; low exposure: Minimal Exposure or Gradient 2. The 1–5 ordinal variable is an analytical recode and is used only as a robustness specification.

## Primary tests
- Within-person paired comparison of conventional and AI/Data domains.
- Occupation-clustered OLS of absolute AI/Data capability on high exposure.
- Occupation-clustered OLS of AI/Data capability on high exposure plus conventional capability.
- Occupation-clustered OLS of capability gap on high exposure.
- Residual AI-readiness test: residuals from AI/Data capability regressed on conventional capability, then tested against high exposure.

## Robustness
High-confidence mappings only; remove baseline-zero case; assessment-date control; current-function controls; HC3 standard errors; ordinal exposure; complete-case demographics; Huber robust regression; influence diagnostics; nonparametric group tests.

## Claim boundaries
- Cross-sectional baseline analyses support association, asymmetry and alignment/misalignment language, not causality.
- Career interest is an aspirational occupation mapping, not observed employment in that occupation.
- ILO exposure is external occupational demand context, not participant-level AI use.
- The post-score n=12 sample cannot identify intervention effects.
- The ten assessment dimensions show a strong general factor, so domain averages are theory-defined indices rather than empirically distinct latent constructs.
