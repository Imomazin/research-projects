# Quantitative Strategic Organization Exemplar

## Primary Methods/Results calibration paper

**Mack, D. Z., Cho, T. S. and Yi, A. C. (2024). “Attentional engagement and strategic responses to discontinuous environmental change: Evidence from the US banking industry.” Strategic Organization, 22(1), 49–90. DOI: 10.1177/14761270231187090.**

This is the preferred quantitative exemplar for the present paper because it is a full empirical Strategic Organization research article using longitudinal US banking data and panel-data methods. It provides a much closer model for empirical Methods, Results, robustness presentation and disciplined interpretation than the conceptual or qualitative exemplars already stored in this folder.

The publisher classifies the article as open access. The repository copy is retained unchanged for non-commercial academic calibration under the article’s Creative Commons licence. Attribution must be preserved and no wording should be copied into our manuscript.

## What to mimic

Use the article to calibrate:

- the speed with which the introduction moves from phenomenon to theoretical problem and contribution;
- the explicit transition from theory to empirical test;
- the density and ordering of the Methods section;
- variable and model exposition before hypothesis tests;
- table-led reporting of main models followed by robustness analysis;
- separation of empirical result from theoretical interpretation;
- limitations that are specific to measurement, design and inference;
- restrained language around association and mechanisms.

## What not to mimic mechanically

Our study is cross-sectional, occupation-linked and individual-level. Mack, Cho and Yi use panel data on banking firms. Their identification strategy, causal language, model family and variable construction therefore cannot be imported into this paper.

For our manuscript, `../POST_AUDIT_CLAIM_RESOLUTION.md` remains authoritative. This exemplar controls **journal-facing empirical writing architecture**, not the substantive claims.
