# World-Class Manuscript Strategy

## 1. Paper identity

Target journal: **Strategic Organization**

Submission type: **Research Article**

Core phenomenon: **strategic capability asymmetry under occupational GenAI exposure**.

The paper combines participant-level capability assessments with an external occupation-level measure of generative-AI exposure. Its strongest contribution is not an intervention effect and not a claim that AI exposure causes a larger gap. The empirical pattern is more specific:

1. Conventional strategic capability is substantially higher than AI/data capability within the same individuals.
2. People targeting occupations with greater GenAI exposure have higher absolute conventional and AI/data capability.
3. Once conventional capability is accounted for, those targeting more exposed occupations do not show an additional relative AI/data capability advantage.
4. The strategic issue is therefore **selective sorting on general capability without commensurate AI-specific readiness**.

Use this as the manuscript's empirical spine.

## 2. Preferred research question

**Do individuals targeting more AI-exposed occupations possess AI/data capability commensurate with their general strategic capability, and what does this reveal about strategic human-capital readiness in an emerging digital economy?**

A secondary formulation may be used in the introduction:

**How does the relationship between conventional strategic capability and AI/data capability vary across occupational GenAI exposure?**

Do not frame the research question causally.

## 3. Construct language

Use the following terms consistently:

- **Conventional strategic capability**: the a priori equal-weight average of Strategic Thinking, Problem Solving, Decision Making, Leadership, Adaptability and Digital Capability.
- **AI/data capability**: the a priori equal-weight average of AI Knowledge, AI Application, AI Judgement and Data Literacy.
- **Capability asymmetry**: conventional strategic capability minus AI/data capability within the same individual.
- **Occupational GenAI exposure**: the ILO 2025 occupation-level exposure category linked through ISCO-08.
- **Relative AI readiness**: AI/data capability evaluated conditional on conventional strategic capability. Treat this as an analytical interpretation, not a validated latent scale.

Do not describe the two domain indices as empirically distinct latent factors. Psychometric analysis shows a strong general factor.

## 4. Contribution architecture

The paper should make a focused strategy/organization contribution, not simply report a skills gap.

### Contribution 1: Strategic human-capital asymmetry

Show that broad strategic capability can coexist with materially weaker AI/data capability. The strategic consequence is that apparently capable talent may still be underprepared for AI-intensive occupational environments.

### Contribution 2: Capability-demand alignment

Use the external ILO exposure taxonomy to introduce an occupation-level demand environment. The important finding is that higher-exposure occupational aspirations attract people with higher absolute capability but do not exhibit a relative AI/data advantage once general capability is considered. This is a misalignment/sorting insight.

### Contribution 3: Human-AI capability theorising

Connect the finding to organization-theory and strategy conversations on human-AI complementarity, organizational learning, strategic human capital, digital transformation and capability development. Keep the contribution at the level justified by the data. Avoid claims about firm performance, organizational outcomes or microfoundational mechanisms that were not observed.

## 5. Recommended title direction

Preferred working title:

**When Strategic Capability Is Not Enough: Occupational AI Exposure and Capability Asymmetry in Emerging Digital Talent**

Strong alternative:

**Strategic Capability Asymmetry in the Age of AI: Occupational Exposure and Human-Capital Readiness**

Avoid “microfoundations” in the final title unless the theory section establishes a precise microfoundational claim that the data can support.

## 6. Introduction strategy

The introduction should move in six deliberate steps:

1. **Phenomenon**: GenAI is changing the capability profile demanded by many occupations and strategic work settings.
2. **Puzzle**: High general strategic/digital capability does not necessarily imply commensurate AI/data capability.
3. **Theoretical gap**: Existing strategy and organization research says much about AI adoption, complementarities and organizational learning, but less about whether individual capability portfolios align with the AI exposure of occupational environments that talent is entering.
4. **Design**: Explain the multi-source linkage of participant capability assessments to ILO 2025 occupational GenAI exposure through ISCO-08 career-interest coding.
5. **Result**: State the large within-person asymmetry and the sorting-without-relative-readiness result clearly.
6. **Contributions**: State two or three bounded theoretical contributions, each connected to a named literature and a specific empirical insight.

Do not begin with generic claims such as “AI is transforming the world.” Avoid consultancy language and inflated novelty claims.

## 7. Theory strategy

Build a compact theoretical frame around three literatures:

### A. Strategic human capital and capability portfolios

Use this literature to explain why the composition and fit of individual capabilities matter strategically, not only their absolute level.

### B. Human-AI complementarity, organizational learning and technology-enabled knowing

Use this literature to explain why AI-related knowledge, application, judgement and data literacy may become complementary to conventional strategic capability.

### C. Occupational exposure and capability-demand alignment

Use the ILO measure as an external demand-context indicator. The theoretical question is not whether exposure directly produces capability. It is whether the capability portfolio of people targeting exposed occupations is aligned with that environment.

Prefer a parsimonious conceptual model over a long list of hypotheses. A research-question / theoretically derived expectations format may fit this study better because the strongest adjusted result is a theoretically informative null.

## 8. Methods strategy

Present the study as a **cross-sectional multi-source capability-demand alignment study**.

Key facts to state exactly:

- Baseline dataset: n=200.
- Career interest available for 198 participants.
- 198 participants mapped to ISCO-08 and ILO 2025 GenAI exposure.
- 19 unique ISCO-08 occupational clusters in the mapped analytic sample.
- High exposure is an analytical grouping of ILO Gradient 3 and Gradient 4, n=125 or 63.13% of the mapped sample.
- Mapping confidence: 145 High, 51 Medium and 2 Low.
- Career interest represents the participant's target/aspirational occupation, not necessarily their current employment.
- ILO exposure represents occupation-level task exposure to GenAI, not participant AI usage or adoption.

Composite construction:

- Conventional strategic capability: equal-weight mean of six baseline dimensions.
- AI/data capability: equal-weight mean of four baseline dimensions.
- Capability asymmetry: conventional minus AI/data capability.
- The ordinal exposure recode is an analytical convenience for robustness only. Do not present it as an official continuous ILO score.

Inference:

- Use occupation-clustered OLS with CR1 small-sample correction and t-based inference because observations share mapped occupations and there are 19 occupation clusters.
- Supplement with Welch/Kruskal-Wallis group tests.
- Report nonparametric within-person tests alongside the paired t-test.
- Report high-confidence-mapping, outlier, assessment-date, job-function-control, HC3, ordinal-exposure, complete-case-demographic, Huber, median-regression, quadratic and interaction robustness checks.

## 9. Measurement discipline

Psychometric results must be used responsibly:

- Conventional six-item alpha ≈ .883.
- AI/data four-item alpha ≈ .945.
- All ten items alpha ≈ .943.
- Conventional and AI/data domain indices correlate ≈ .919.
- PCA first eigenvalue ≈ 7.142 and second ≈ .658.
- Parallel analysis retains one component.

Implication: the ten dimensions contain a dominant general capability factor. The paper may use the two a priori domain averages because they are theoretically meaningful, but it must not claim that the data establish two clean latent constructs. Capability asymmetry is best described as a within-person contrast between theory-defined domains.

Also disclose substantial ceiling effects in several conventional dimensions.

## 10. Main empirical narrative

### Finding 1: large capability asymmetry

Mapped n=198:

- Mean conventional capability = 86.43.
- Mean AI/data capability = 69.80.
- Mean within-person gap = 16.63 points.
- Bootstrap 95% CI for mean gap = 15.80 to 17.43.
- Median gap = 17.48.
- Paired t(197) = 39.58, p < .001.
- Wilcoxon p < .001.
- Cohen dz = 2.81, bootstrap 95% CI approximately 2.47 to 3.27.

### Finding 2: absolute capability sorting by occupational exposure

Exposure group means:

- Minimal, n=9: conventional 72.04; AI/data 59.84; gap 12.19.
- Gradient 2, n=64: conventional 81.91; AI/data 66.00; gap 15.91.
- Gradient 3, n=67: conventional 89.32; AI/data 72.10; gap 17.22.
- Gradient 4, n=58: conventional 90.30; AI/data 72.87; gap 17.44.

High exposure predicts higher absolute AI/data capability in the unadjusted occupation-clustered model:

- B = 7.21, SE = 1.01, p < .001.

### Finding 3: no relative AI/data advantage conditional on general capability

When conventional capability is included:

- High-exposure coefficient on AI/data capability B = 0.11, SE = 1.12, p = .921.
- Conventional capability B ≈ .782, p < .001.
- Model R² ≈ .844.

Capability-gap model:

- High exposure B ≈ 1.87 unadjusted, p ≈ .218.
- High exposure B ≈ -0.11 after conventional capability is included, p ≈ .921.

Residual relative-readiness model:

- High exposure B ≈ 0.10, p ≈ .930.

Interpretation: people targeting more exposed occupations are stronger in absolute terms, but their AI/data capability is not disproportionately stronger once their conventional capability is taken into account.

## 11. Robustness narrative

The adjusted exposure result remains null when:

- only High-confidence occupation mappings are retained;
- the baseline-zero case is removed;
- assessment date is added;
- current job function controls are added;
- HC3 standard errors are used;
- ordinal ILO exposure replaces the high-exposure binary;
- a complete-case demographic specification is used;
- Huber robust regression is used;
- median quantile regression is used;
- nonlinear conventional capability is allowed;
- a high-exposure × conventional-capability interaction is tested.

Assessment date shows no meaningful correlation with exposure, conventional capability, AI/data capability or the gap.

Do not bury the null result. It is central to the sorting/alignment interpretation.

## 12. Post-assessment discipline

Only 12 participants have an overall post score. Component-level post measures are absent and intervention fields are largely missing. Three reassessments occur on the same day and one participant has a baseline of zero.

Observed exploratory gain:

- n=12 mean gain ≈ 27.63.
- median gain ≈ 27.56.
- all 12 gains are positive.
- Wilcoxon p ≈ .00049.

Sensitivity analyses remain positive after excluding baseline zero and/or same-day observations.

However, this evidence is **appendix-only and noncausal**. The paper must not claim intervention effectiveness. If space is tight, omit it from the main manuscript entirely and retain only a supplementary note.

## 13. Writing strategy

Every substantive paragraph should normally do four things:

1. make one analytical or theoretical claim;
2. identify the relevant literature or evidence;
3. explain the mechanism/logic;
4. connect the point to the paper's argument.

Style rules:

- Active, precise academic prose.
- No generic AI hype.
- No rhetorical padding.
- No repetitive signposting.
- Avoid strings of citations without synthesis.
- Avoid unexplained buzzwords.
- Use one term for one construct throughout.
- Distinguish evidence, interpretation and speculation.
- Treat null results as information, not embarrassment.
- Keep contribution claims proportional to a cross-sectional individual-level design.

Use the exemplar papers for pacing, argument architecture, paragraph density and movement from phenomenon to theory to contribution. Never imitate distinctive sentences or phrasing.

## 14. Citation and reference strategy

Use **SAGE Harvard** throughout.

Citation hierarchy:

1. Foundational strategic human-capital / capability work.
2. Strong strategy and organization scholarship on AI, digital transformation, human-AI complementarity, organizational learning and knowing.
3. Recent work from Strategic Organization itself.
4. Adjacent leading journals: Strategic Management Journal, Organization Science, Academy of Management Journal, Academy of Management Review, Administrative Science Quarterly, Journal of Management Studies and selected high-quality digital-strategy outlets.
5. ILO 2025 as the authoritative source for occupational GenAI exposure.
6. High-quality emerging-economy/context literature only where it does theoretical work.

Reference quality rules:

- Never fabricate a citation, DOI, volume, issue or page range.
- Verify every bibliographic record against the publisher/Crossref/official source.
- Use the peer-reviewed version where available.
- Do not cite preprints if a journal version exists.
- Avoid weak blogs, consultancy reports and generic web commentary in the scholarly argument.
- Prefer a focused reference list, approximately 55–75 genuinely used sources, over citation inflation.
- Aim for a substantial contemporary layer from 2020–2026 while retaining the classic theoretical anchors.
- Ensure Strategic Organization is visibly part of the intellectual conversation without gaming the reference list.

## 15. Main tables strategy

Keep the main paper visually disciplined. Recommended main tables:

**Table 1. Sample, measurement and occupation-mapping profile**
- baseline n, mapped n, key demographics where sufficiently complete;
- composite definitions;
- mapping confidence and exposure distribution.

**Table 2. Descriptive statistics, reliability and correlations**
- means, SDs and correlations for core analytical variables;
- reliability metrics in a compact note or panel.

**Table 3. Capability profile by ILO GenAI exposure group**
- n, conventional mean, AI/data mean and within-person gap by exposure category;
- concise omnibus tests.

**Table 4. Core occupation-clustered regression models**
- absolute AI/data capability;
- adjusted AI/data capability;
- capability gap;
- residual relative AI readiness.

Move detailed robustness, mapping checks, psychometric diagnostics, influence diagnostics and post-assessment sensitivity results to the online supplement.

Tables must be self-contained. Define all abbreviations in notes. Use consistent decimals. Report exact p-values where practical, with p < .001 for very small values. Do not duplicate every table value in prose.

## 16. Figures strategy

Recommended main figures:

**Figure 1. Conceptual/analytic model**
An original figure showing conventional strategic capability and AI/data capability as a capability portfolio situated against occupational GenAI exposure. This must be newly designed, not copied from an exemplar.

**Figure 2. Within-person strategic capability asymmetry**
A clean publication figure showing conventional versus AI/data capability and the magnitude of the within-person difference.

**Figure 3. Capability sorting across occupational GenAI exposure**
Show how absolute conventional and AI/data capability rise across exposure groups while the relative relationship remains broadly stable.

Optional supplemental figures:
- exposure distribution;
- parallel analysis;
- influence/robustness visualization if genuinely useful.

Figure rules:

- 300 dpi minimum.
- No 3-D charts, decorative icons or unnecessary backgrounds.
- Use colourblind-safe distinctions and ensure grayscale readability.
- Keep fonts, axis formats and terminology consistent across figures.
- Give captions an analytical message, not merely a label.

## 17. Discussion strategy

The discussion should proceed in this order:

1. restate the empirical answer without repeating the results section;
2. explain why capability asymmetry matters theoretically;
3. explain the sorting-without-relative-readiness result;
4. connect the findings to human-AI complementarity and strategic human capital;
5. specify boundary conditions;
6. discuss implications for capability development and organizational talent systems cautiously;
7. state limitations transparently;
8. close with a concise forward-looking theoretical implication.

Do not turn the discussion into managerial recommendations detached from the data.

## 18. Limitations that must be explicit

- Cross-sectional design.
- Predominantly Nigerian sample and limited geographic generalisability.
- Career interest is aspirational and may not equal eventual occupation.
- ILO exposure is occupation-level and not individual AI usage.
- Original capability scoring instrument requires careful description and its external validity should not be overstated.
- Ceiling effects in several conventional dimensions.
- Strong general-factor structure limits claims that the two domain composites are empirically distinct constructs.
- Missing demographic fields reduce complete-case control samples.
- Only 12 overall post scores and no credible causal intervention design.

These limitations should sharpen the boundary of the contribution, not be hidden.

## 19. Data governance and transparency

The GitHub repository is private because it contains participant-level data.

Do not recommend public release of participant-level raw data without a formal disclosure, anonymisation and ethics/governance review.

A suitable manuscript data-availability position is that analysis code, non-disclosive aggregate outputs and documentation can be made available subject to project governance, while participant-level data are restricted because of confidentiality and ethical constraints.

## 20. Final quality test

The paper is ready only when:

- the contribution is visibly at the strategy–organization intersection;
- the main result is reproduced exactly from the analysis files;
- no causal claim is made from the cross-sectional exposure models or n=12 follow-up;
- every citation is real and verified;
- every table/figure reconciles to the analysis output;
- the abstract is 100–150 words;
- there are at least five keywords;
- total manuscript word count remains below the journal guidance;
- SAGE Harvard is applied consistently;
- the review manuscript is fully anonymised;
- all figures are publication quality at 300 dpi;
- the paper reads as a coherent theoretical argument rather than a data report.
